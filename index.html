I build AI that does things, and work on keeping it safe. I'm a computer science student at IIIT Bangalore, currently a visiting researcher at SUTD in Singapore, where I use language models to find bugs and vulnerabilities in software and in machine-learning systems.

Before this I built an agentic AI system for network operations at Microsoft, shipped AI apps now in production at Trimble, applied machine learning to brain imaging at IIIT Bangalore, and won the SEBI National Securities Market Hackathon with an AI fraud detector.

What I'm working on now

LLM-guided fuzzing of serialized ML models. Model files like pickles can carry executable payloads. At SUTD I'm reproducing systems such as ShadowPickle, PickleFuzzer and DynaHug/DynaOrg, building Docker-based baselines, and exploring how semantic-aware mutation, constraint solving and LLM reasoning can push fuzzers toward deeper vulnerabilities in binary and serialized formats.

More broadly I'm drawn to AI that actually does things — agents that reason, use tools and act on other systems — and to what goes wrong when you give models more autonomy. Fintech and fraud detection are my other recurring rabbit hole.

FeedbackFuzz

Research prototype, 2026

Extends HLPFuzz (USENIX Security 2025), which uses an LLM to solve the constraints that block a fuzzer. FeedbackFuzz classifies each failure as a syntax, type or semantic error and feeds the model a structured template for that error type, cutting solving iterations by 27.9% across the test scenarios.
